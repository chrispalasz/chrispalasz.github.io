Vertical & Horizontal Alignment

01 / Challenge

The learning or performance need

A multi-year English program needed a coherent view of how concepts, texts, performance tasks, criteria, and transferable skills worked together within units and increased in complexity across grade levels.

Design response

I contributed to a collaborative planning process spanning grades 6-10, incorporated feedback from colleagues, program coordinators, and students, and helped map recurring skills such as citation, intertextuality, connotation, comparison, and essay structure.

02 / Design decisions

How the experience was shaped

01

Align within each unit

Concepts, contexts, texts, assessments, criteria, and transfer links are presented as one planning system rather than isolated fields.

02

Show progression across years

Recurring skills increase in complexity from introductory work through more independent analysis and disciplinary transfer.

03

Vary performance evidence

Learners create analytical, creative, oral, visual, and digital products for different audiences and purposes.

04

Preserve honest gaps

The portfolio edition labels fields that were not documented in the source plan rather than inventing missing instructional decisions.

03 / Selected evidence

Inside the work

04 / Evaluation

Collaboration and portfolio treatment

A future iteration should connect each mapped progression more explicitly to common performance evidence and review the sequence against current program priorities and learner results.

  • The source plan includes 20 planned units across five grade-level sequences.
  • The map documents cross-unit skill progression and interdisciplinary transfer opportunities.
  • The work was collaborative; this case study does not claim sole authorship of the curriculum.
  • The portfolio edition improves readability and consistency while preserving source-plan decisions.
