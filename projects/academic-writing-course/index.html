Course design

Professional & Academic English Writing

Four-Week Independent-Study Course

An intensive independent-study course that scaffolds multilingual university learners from source use and thesis development through revision, presentation, and reflection.

View complete sampleContact me
Audience
Chinese university students preparing for English-medium academic work
My role
Needs researcher, curriculum designer, content developer, and assessment designer
Deliverable
Four-week course and curated portfolio edition
Year
2020 / Portfolio edition 2026

01 / Challenge

The learning or performance need

The target learners had substantial experience studying English but varied experience with source-supported academic writing. Because the course was designed for independent study, learners could not rely on routine instructor explanations, reminders, or individualized feedback.

Design response

I divided one complex writing project into visible daily deliverables, predictable reflection prompts, weekly checklists, staged drafting, delayed final revision, and multiple opportunities for self- and peer-generated feedback.

02 / Design decisions

How the experience was shaped

01

Build one authentic project

Daily work contributes to a source-supported essay, presentation, and reflective portfolio rather than a collection of unrelated exercises.

02

Make progress visible

Each study day identifies one required product so learners can tell what finished means without instructor clarification.

03

Align practice and evidence

Diagnostic, formative, collaborative, and summative evidence appears throughout the process rather than only at the end.

04

Avoid over-modeling

Model-light supports provide decision criteria and processes without supplying complete language that learners can simply imitate.

03 / Selected evidence

Inside the work

04 / Evaluation

Design basis and limitations

A future implementation should pilot the course and collect completion, revision, and learner-experience evidence before making impact claims.

  • Development was informed by a designer-conducted survey of more than 800 Chinese university students about self-study habits.
  • The course draws on self-regulated learning, process writing, project-based learning, and academic-writing research.
  • The source document does not report formal completion or learning-outcome data.
  • The 2026 edition documents accessibility, citation, and responsible-AI improvements for a current implementation.
