Faculty Onboarding & Teaching Resource Guide

Supporting English-Medium Instruction

A faculty-facing orientation and performance-support resource designed to help instructors prepare for English-medium university courses.

01 / Challenge

The learning or performance need

Capable instructors could arrive with subject expertise but little context for how English-medium teaching operated at the institution. They needed quick orientation before teaching and practical guidance while planning or facilitating a class.

Design response

I organized scattered contextual knowledge into a pathway that orients instructors, anticipates recurring decisions, translates advice into observable actions, and supports both initial onboarding and later just-in-time reference.

02 / Design decisions

How the experience was shaped

01

Organize by performance need

Institutional orientation is separated from participation, scaffolding, language-skill practice, and resource selection.

02

Translate advice into action

Examples, routines, activity steps, prompts, and checklists replace long explanatory passages where immediate use matters.

03

Design for multiple use moments

The resource supports preparation before the first class and quick reference during later planning.

04

Treat content as maintainable

Links, platform guidance, and institutional context require owners, review dates, and periodic subject-matter validation.

03 / Selected evidence

Inside the work

04 / Evaluation

Evidence base and boundaries

A stronger next iteration would begin with interviews, prioritize the highest-friction onboarding decisions, and measure findability, confidence, task completion, and examples of classroom application.

  • The original work drew on classroom experience, institutional context, and contributions from faculty colleagues and students.
  • The portfolio edition reconstructs key content as scannable job aids and checklists.
  • The project did not include a formal needs-analysis dataset or measured instructor outcomes.
  • Institution-specific claims and older resource links require review before reuse.
